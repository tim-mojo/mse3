Future Ready Maryland: Accelerate AI

One statewide AI readiness Constellation, from the workforce to the classroom to the community.

About this Constellation

Future Ready Maryland: Accelerate AI brings MSE3’s AI work together into a single Constellation that addresses the full continuum of AI readiness: literacy, proficiency, fluency, and competency, for Marylanders at every stage of life. It is organized around three components. A1, AI in the Workforce, helps Maryland employers upskill the employees they already have. A2, AI in K-20 Education, prepares students, educators, and faculty from kindergarten through college. A3, Ignite AI: Communities and Community Organizations, brings AI experiences and resources to the public through Nonprofits, Museums and Informal Science Institutions, and Community Service Organizations.

The Maryland AI Coordination Hub is the umbrella over all three, providing the shared infrastructure, strategy, and coordination that connect them. Together they advance the ethical, equitable, and responsible use of AI across the state, with a particular commitment to the Not-Yet-Reached.

Key partners include the Maryland Department of Commerce, the Maryland Department of Labor, the Maryland State Department of Education (MSDE), the Maryland Association of Community Colleges, the Maryland Center for Computing Education (MCCE), state legislators, the Greater Baltimore Committee (GBC), TEDCO, and industry leaders, joined by a growing network of MSE3 Partners.

The Maryland AI Coordination Hub

The umbrella for A1, A2, and A3

The Maryland AI Coordination Hub is not a fourth component. It is the umbrella that encompasses all three, serving as Maryland’s statewide coordinating backbone for AI literacy, adoption, workforce readiness, and capacity building across the workforce, education, and communities.

The Hub is organized around five responsibilities, each serving one or more components:

  • AI Learning and Resource Navigator A1 · A2 · A3A public platform built with EcoMap Technologies that maps AI resources across all three components and connects employers, workers, students, educators, and community members to tailored pathways.
  • Maryland AI Readiness Strategic Plan A1 · A2 · A3Aligned with the state’s AI Enablement Strategy and developed with DoIT, the Maryland Department of Labor, and the Governor’s Office.
  • AI Deployment Support A1Technical assistance for small businesses, entrepreneurs, and local governments through partners such as TEDCO, UpSurge, MOST, GBC, and NPower, along with specified, industry-appropriate training delivered through community colleges.
  • AI Readiness Training and Capacity Building A1 · A2Shared frameworks and portable credentials across employers, schools, and colleges, including the Physical AI / Robotics Challenge pilot that connects to Robotics is a Sport in Maryland.
  • Coordination Within Priority Sectors A1Convenings across Maryland’s lighthouse industries of information technology, life sciences, aerospace and defense, and manufacturing.
Governance. MSE3 guides the Hub. The MSE3 Council, together with its Industry Advisory Board and Academic Advisory Board, sets the Hub’s direction and priorities. The Hub’s progress is measured through the outcomes of its three components, A1, A2, and A3, described below.

A1. AI in the Workforce

Upskilling Maryland’s employees

Helps Maryland businesses and public employers meet a growing need: targeted AI upskilling for the employees they already have. Led by the Industry and Government sectors in partnership with Academia, A1 pairs Maryland community colleges with specific business sectors to deliver training tailored to the jobs, products, and services of each industry.

This training is intentionally practical rather than credential driven. Community colleges are the delivery partner because their workforce and continuing education divisions can design focused, noncredit programs quickly and alongside employers. The goal is not to credential employees, but to help them learn to use and engage with the AI solutions relevant to their jobs and to their industry’s products and services. Training covers:

  • Using AI on the JobApplying AI tools to the everyday tasks, workflows, and decisions of a specific role or industry.
  • Choosing the Right ToolComparing and selecting among leading AI platforms, such as Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot, based on the task, the organization’s existing technology, and its data policies.
  • Building with AIDeveloping simple apps and AI agents that automate tasks or support the industry’s products and services.
  • Responsible UseData privacy, security, accuracy, and company policy when using AI in the workplace.

Training is delivered in sector cohorts, beginning with Maryland’s lighthouse industries of information technology, life sciences, aerospace and defense, and manufacturing, with employers co-designing content around their real workflows. A1 connects to the -Ship Yard, where new AI-related roles create new -Ships.

Measurable outcomes

  • An increase in the number of sector cohorts pairing a community college with an industry sector, beginning with Maryland’s lighthouse industries.
  • An increase in the number of community colleges offering employer-partnered AI upskilling programs.
  • An increase in the number of participating employers and the number of employees completing AI upskilling.
  • An increase in the number of AI use cases, apps, or agents put into practice by participating employees.
  • An increase in employer-reported time savings or productivity gains attributable to the training.
  • An increase in the number of small businesses, entrepreneurs, and local governments receiving AI Deployment Support, and in estimated small business savings from AI adoption.
  • An increase in priority sector convenings across Maryland’s lighthouse industries.
  • An increase in workforce AI upskilling opportunities listed in the Hub’s AI Learning and Resource Navigator.

A2. AI in K-20 Education

Schools, colleges, and universities

Supports Maryland’s education pipeline from kindergarten through college in building AI literacy and hands-on AI skills for students, and in preparing educators and faculty to use AI responsibly in teaching and learning. For school systems, this means adopting formal AI strategies for teaching, learning, workforce readiness, and organizational effectiveness, aligned with the Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act (2026) and MSDE guidance. For colleges and universities, including community colleges, it means expanding AI curriculum, credit-bearing coursework, and certifications that prepare students for AI-relevant careers.

Schools and campuses can also draw on the Hour of AI and Partner resources offered through A3. This component builds on the inaugural AI for Education Summit that MSE3 hosted with MCCE and Google in June 2025.

Measurable outcomes

  • An increase in the number of Maryland school systems that have adopted and implemented a formal AI strategy, toward at least 25 percent (6 or more of 24).
  • An annual AI for Education Summit convening school systems, higher education, industry, and policymakers.
  • Educator professional development connected to portable AI micro-credentials recognized across the P-20 pipeline through the Hub.
  • An increase in the percentage of Maryland colleges and universities offering AI curriculum, measured against a baseline.
  • Expanded availability of AI certifications and pre-college AI credits, such as dual enrollment, for students in Not-Yet-Reached communities.
  • Development of statewide student AI competitions in hardware (robotics) and software (machine learning and AI applications), beginning with the Physical AI / Robotics Challenge pilot.
  • An increase in K-20 AI programs and resources listed in the Hub’s AI Learning and Resource Navigator.

A3. Ignite AI

Communities and community organizations

Brings AI to Marylanders where they live, gather, and learn outside of school. A3 is led by the Nonprofits, Museums and Informal Science Institutions, and Community Service Organizations sectors, which play two roles:

  • Providers of AI ExperiencesMuseums, science centers, libraries, recreation centers, and community organizations host public AI experiences such as exhibits, workshops, family nights, and Hour of AI events, using the turnkey resources, training, and implementation guides provided by Code.org and MSE3 Partner organizations. These give students, families, and adults safe, ethical, and engaging first experiences with AI and serve as the on-ramp to deeper learning through A1 and A2.
  • Adopters of AINonprofits and community organizations learn to use AI to better deliver their own programs, products, and services, from outreach and operations to program design and impact reporting, making them stronger partners across the ecosystem.

Measurable outcomes

  • Hour of AI events hosted in all 24 Maryland jurisdictions each school year.
  • An increase in registered Hour of AI host sites statewide, toward 250 sites including at least 25 libraries and recreation centers.
  • Every host site listed and discoverable through MSE3 and the Hub’s AI Learning and Resource Navigator.
  • An increase in the number of museums and informal science institutions offering AI exhibits or AI experiences, measured against a baseline.
  • An increase in the number of nonprofits and community organizations using AI to deliver their programs and services, measured against a baseline.
  • An increase in the number of Marylanders connected to AI literacy pathways through community AI experiences.
